Why Automatic Mixed Precision (AMP) is non-negotiable here. Training in full FP32 on this GPU would push activations alone past 5 GB, leaving no room for gradients. AMP stores weights in FP32 but computes forward/backward passes in FP16, cutting activation memory by ~40% while introducing less than 0.1% accuracy degradation on classification tasks. It is always on for this run (amp=True).
⚠️
If you encounter a CUDA Out of Memory error despite these settings, reduce batch from 16 → 12 or 8. The run uses imgsz=320 at batch=16, which keeps peak VRAM roughly equal to the previous 224 px / batch 32 configuration; memory scales quadratically with image size.

02 Model Selection Rationale

✓ Selected Architecture

YOLO11l-cls  ·  yolo11l-cls.pt

Ultralytics YOLO11 Large, pre-trained on ImageNet-1k (1,000 classes, 1.28M images). ~25M parameters. Backbone: CSPDarkNet with C3k2 modules + SPPF + Partial Self-Attention.

The decision between YOLO variants is not arbitrary. Below is the explicit reasoning that eliminated every other candidate before YOLO11l-cls was selected.

Why not EfficientNet / ResNet / ViT?

These are excellent classifiers but would require stepping outside the Ultralytics ecosystem, losing built-in augmentation pipelines, the training loop, mixed-precision handling, and the hierarchical model integration already planned downstream. The accuracy delta between a well-tuned YOLO11l-cls and EfficientNet-B4 on a custom food dataset is <1% — not worth the integration overhead.

Why YOLO11l over YOLOv8l?

YOLOv8l-cls was the original candidate. YOLO11 (released October 2024) supersedes it with the same weight file format and identical Ultralytics API, making it a zero-cost upgrade. The architecture improvements are concentrated in the backbone:

  • C3k2 blocks replace C2f — deeper bottleneck with smaller kernel cross-stage connections, improving gradient flow on fine-grained features (food texture, colour gradients).
  • Partial Self-Attention (PSA) in the neck gives the model limited global context without the quadratic cost of full ViT attention, helping distinguish visually similar classes (e.g., pancakes vs. waffles).
  • On Ultralytics' internal ImageNet benchmarks, YOLO11l-cls achieves +0.9% Top-1 vs YOLOv8l-cls at identical VRAM usage.

Why not YOLO11x-cls or YOLOv8x-cls?

The -x (Extra-Large) variants have ~56M parameters. At batch=32 and imgsz=224 they already consume approximately 9.4 GB VRAM under AMP — exceeding the 8 GB physical limit — and the move to imgsz=320 used here makes the gap worse. Shrinking the batch far enough to fit would introduce noisier gradient estimates that hurt accuracy on a 132-class dataset. The accuracy gain over YOLO11l is only ~0.4% on this type of dataset.

03 Combating Domain Shift

⚠ The Domain Shift Problem

Training images are clean web-sourced food photos (professional lighting, centred framing, high resolution). Inference images are smartphone photos taken by students inside classrooms. Without targeted augmentation, a model trained on clean data can lose 15–30% Top-1 accuracy when faced with classroom conditions — effectively making it useless for the intended application.

The augmentation strategy is designed to synthetically simulate classroom conditions during every training batch, forcing the model to become invariant to artefacts it will encounter at inference time. Each augmentation maps to a specific real-world condition:

💡
Harsh / Fluorescent Lighting
hsv_s=0.75 · hsv_v=0.50 · hsv_h=0.02
Randomly shifts the HSV colour space independently. High hsv_v (±50% brightness) simulates overexposed windows, flickering fluorescent tubes, and deep shadows cast by tray edges. High hsv_s handles the washed-out de-saturation of fluorescent light and the oversaturation of phone HDR processing.
📐
Odd Camera Angles & Tilts
degrees=20 · shear=8 · flipud=0.15 · fliplr=0.5
Students rarely hold phones perfectly horizontal. ±20° rotation covers photos taken portrait while the tray is landscape, or vice versa. Shear and vertical flip simulate photos taken from the side of the tray or from an elevated angle looking down.
📷
Perspective Distortion
perspective=0.0008 · scale=0.60
Cheap smartphone lenses introduce projective distortion, especially at close range. The perspective transform warps the image plane to simulate this. Scale jitter (±60%) covers both extreme close-ups (one dish filling the frame) and wide shots (entire cafeteria tray with multiple items).
🌀
Motion Blur & Focus Loss
MotionBlur · Defocus · GaussianBlur (35% p)
Injected via Albumentations callback (not natively in Ultralytics). Simulates hand-shake (MotionBlur, kernel 3–9px), slow autofocus (Defocus, radius 1–5px), and low-resolution upscaling (GaussianBlur). Applied stochastically to 35% of all training batches.
🙈
Partial Occlusion
erasing=0.40
Randomly erases rectangular patches covering up to 40% of the image area. Simulates a student's hand partially entering frame, sauce or wrapping obscuring the food, or another tray overlapping. Forces the model to rely on local texture rather than whole-object shape.
🔀
Similar-Class Confusion
mixup=0.10 · label_smoothing=0.10
MixUp blends two training images and their labels, hardening decision boundaries between visually similar classes (e.g., bibimbap vs. fried rice). Label smoothing prevents the model from being overconfident on ambiguous training examples, which directly improves calibration at inference time.
🚫
Why Mosaic augmentation is disabled (mosaic=0.0). Mosaic was invented for multi-object detection tasks — it tiles four images together to expose the detector to objects at unusual scales and positions. For single-label classification, it creates nonsensical composite images (four different foods in one frame with a single label) that confuse the classifier and degrade accuracy. It is explicitly set to 0 in this training run.

04 Full Augmentation Reference

Parameter Value Rationale & Domain Shift Mapping
hsv_h 0.02
±2% hue rotation. Models subtle colour temperature shift between warm incandescent classroom lights and cold fluorescent/LED strips. Low value keeps food colours plausible.
hsv_s 0.75
±75% saturation. High range covers the full spectrum from washed-out fluorescent de-saturation to phone camera over-processing/HDR saturation boost.
hsv_v 0.50
±50% brightness. Critical for handling harsh overhead lights, window reflections on glossy food surfaces, and deep shadows cast by stacked trays.
degrees 20.0
±20° random rotation. Covers photos taken with the phone tilted or sideways. Food does not have a canonical orientation the model should rely on.
translate 0.15
±15% random XY translation. Food is rarely perfectly centred; students often shoot quickly without composing the frame.
scale 0.60
±60% scale jitter. Covers extreme close-ups where only part of a dish is visible, through to wide shots of an entire tray. Largest single source of real-world variance.
shear 8.0
±8° shear. Approximates the perspective-like distortion from off-axis shots (holding the phone slightly to the side of the tray rather than directly above).
perspective 0.0008
Projective warp coefficient. Simulates the lens barrel distortion and vanishing-point convergence of smartphone cameras, especially at close range.
flipud 0.15
15% vertical flip. Uncommon but occurs when a phone is held upside-down by younger students, or when photos are auto-rotated incorrectly.
fliplr 0.50
50% horizontal flip. Standard spatial invariance — food appearance does not depend on left-right orientation.
erasing 0.40
40% probability of random rectangular erasure. Simulates hands, utensils, wrappers, or adjacent trays partially occluding the target food item.
mixup 0.10
10% image pair blending. Hardens decision boundaries between similar food classes. Especially useful for cuisine pairs that share ingredients (fried rice variants, pasta types).
mosaic 0.0
Disabled. Detection-era augmentation that degrades single-label classification accuracy by creating semantically invalid composite images.

(albumentations)		 p=0.35
Applied via custom callback. Kernel size 3–9 px simulates hand-shake during capture — the most common smartphone artefact in casual, quick photography.
Defocus
(albumentations)		 p=0.35
Radius 1–5 px. Simulates slow autofocus — very common when pointing a phone at food on a table (camera AF hunts between food and background).

Step 2 — Launch Training

Python train_general_model.py 
from ultralytics import YOLO
import albumentations as A
import torch, numpy as np

blur_transform = A.Compose([A.OneOf([
    A.MotionBlur(blur_limit=(3, 9), p=1.0),
    A.Defocus(radius=(1, 5), alias_blur=0.1, p=1.0),
    A.GaussianBlur(blur_limit=(3, 7), p=1.0),
], p=0.35)])

def apply_blur_augmentation(trainer):
    if hasattr(trainer, "batch") and trainer.batch is not None:
        imgs = trainer.batch["img"]
        imgs_np = (imgs.permute(0,2,3,1).cpu().numpy() * 255).astype("uint8")
        augmented = [blur_transform(image=img)["image"] for img in imgs_np]
        trainer.batch["img"] = torch.from_numpy(
            np.stack(augmented).transpose(0,3,1,2).astype("float32") / 255.0
        ).to(imgs.device)

model = YOLO("yolo11l-cls.pt")
model.add_callback("on_train_batch_start", apply_blur_augmentation)

results = model.train(
    data       = r"E:\final project - models retrain\datasets\general_model_flattened",
    task       = "classify",
    epochs     = 150,  imgsz = 320,  batch  = 16,
    workers    = 4,    device = 0,    amp    = True,   cache = False,
    optimizer  = "AdamW",  lr0 = 0.001,  lrf = 0.01,  cos_lr = True,
    momentum   = 0.937,  weight_decay = 0.0005,
    warmup_epochs = 5,  warmup_momentum = 0.8,
    dropout = 0.2,  label_smoothing = 0.1,
    patience = 30,  save = True,  save_period = 10,
    # ── Domain-shift augmentations ──────────────────────────────
    hsv_h = 0.02,   hsv_s = 0.75,    hsv_v = 0.50,
    degrees = 20.0, translate = 0.15, scale = 0.60,
    shear = 8.0,   perspective = 0.0008,
    flipud = 0.15, fliplr = 0.50,
    erasing = 0.40, mixup = 0.10, mosaic = 0.0,
    # ── Output ─────────────────────────────────────────────────
    project = r"E:\final project - models retrain\runs",
    name = "general_model_flattened",  pretrained = True,
)

06 Every Parameter Explained

Core Training Parameters

Parameter Value Explanation
task "classify" Instructs Ultralytics to use the classification head (softmax over N classes) rather than the detection head (bounding boxes + objectness). This changes the loss function, metrics, and output format.
epochs 150
Maximum training iterations over the full dataset. 150 gives a pretrained YOLO11l ample gradient steps to adapt its head and fine-tune the backbone for 132 food classes at the higher 320 px resolution. Early stopping (patience=30) will terminate earlier if the model converges.
imgsz 320
Input resolution in pixels (320×320), raised from 224. Higher resolution is the single largest accuracy lever for fine-grained food classification — it preserves the fine texture and colour detail that distinguishes visually similar dishes. The cost is quadratically higher activation memory, absorbed here by reducing batch to 16.
batch 16
Images processed per gradient step. Reduced from 32 to 16 to fund the move to 320 px within the 8 GB VRAM budget under AMP. The cosine LR schedule and 5-epoch warmup compensate for the slightly noisier gradient estimates of the smaller batch.
workers 4
Number of CPU threads for data loading and augmentation. Set to 4 for Windows compatibility — the Windows multiprocessing model requires lower worker counts than Linux to avoid deadlocks in PyTorch DataLoader. Increasing to 8 may cause training hangs on Windows.
device 0 GPU device index. 0 = first GPU (RTX 3060 Ti). Use "cpu" for CPU fallback (not recommended — training will be 20–50× slower).
amp True Automatic Mixed Precision. Runs forward/backward passes in FP16, stores master weights in FP32. Reduces activation memory by ~40%, enabling batch=16 at imgsz=320 on 8 GB VRAM. Accuracy impact is negligible (<0.1% Top-1).
cache False Dataset image caching. Disabled because caching the full dataset in RAM or VRAM would exhaust available memory on this hardware. With SSD storage, the I/O bottleneck from disk is minimal.
pretrained True Load ImageNet-pretrained backbone weights. This is the single most important accuracy decision — transfer learning from ImageNet dramatically reduces the number of epochs needed to achieve high food classification accuracy.

Optimizer Parameters

Parameter Value Explanation
optimizer "AdamW" AdamW applies weight decay correctly (decoupled from gradient scaling), unlike SGD+momentum. For fine-tuning pretrained models on complex multi-class tasks, AdamW consistently outperforms SGD by 0.5–1.5% Top-1.
lr0 0.001 Initial learning rate. Conservative relative to training-from-scratch (which would use 0.01). Low LR is critical for fine-tuning — too high and you destroy the pretrained backbone features in the first few epochs.
lrf 0.01 Final LR multiplier (cosine schedule). Final LR = lr0 × lrf = 0.001 × 0.01 = 0.00001. The cosine decay gradually reduces the learning rate, allowing the model to settle into a sharp, accurate minimum rather than oscillating around it.
momentum 0.937 AdamW beta1 (exponential moving average of gradients). Standard value from the AdamW paper; controls how much momentum from previous gradient steps influences the current update.
weight_decay 0.0005 L2 regularisation coefficient. Prevents individual weights from growing too large, reducing overfitting on the food training set. Especially important given the aggressive augmentations — it works synergistically to prevent memorisation.
warmup_epochs 5 Linearly ramps LR from ~0 to lr0 over the first 5 epochs. Prevents catastrophic forgetting — if the full LR hits the pretrained backbone at epoch 1, early gradient updates can permanently damage feature representations that took ImageNet training weeks to learn.
warmup_momentum 0.8 Initial momentum during warmup phase. Ramps from 0.8 to the target momentum value (0.937) over warmup epochs, providing an additional layer of gradient stabilisation during the critical early training phase.

Regularisation Parameters

Parameter Value Explanation
dropout 0.2 Randomly zeros 20% of neurons in the classification head during each forward pass. Prevents the final layer from co-adapting to specific training image quirks. Helps generalise to the smartphone image distribution at inference time.
label_smoothing 0.1 Replaces hard one-hot labels (e.g., [0,0,1,0,0]) with soft labels (e.g., [0.0007,0.0007,0.9,0.0007,…]). Forces the model to maintain residual probability for similar classes, preventing overconfidence on visually ambiguous food images and improving calibration.
patience 30 Early stopping: training halts if Top-1 validation accuracy does not improve for 30 consecutive epochs. Prevents wasted compute on a converged model, and avoids overfitting the augmented training distribution.
save_period 10 Saves a full checkpoint every 10 epochs in addition to the running best.pt. Acts as insurance — if training crashes at epoch 90, you can resume from epoch 80 rather than restarting from scratch.
